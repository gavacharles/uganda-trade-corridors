## Post 1 · the hook — media: rx1_two_corridors.mp4

Same truck. Same 440 km. Same tarmac.

Nairobi → Ugandan border: 3+ hours lost to the roadside.
Pretoria → Zimbabwe border: under 1.5.

The difference? 73,000 buildings along the Kenyan road. 8,900 along the South African one.

East Africa's trade roads have become towns.

## Post 3 · linear towns — media: rx9_road_type_reveal.mp4

Two-thirds of East Africa's main-road length now runs through roadside settlement or town. In Southern Africa: one-third.

Across 54 roads, roadside building alone tracks lost time (r = 0.73).

It's not the road. It's what's been built along it.

## Post 4 · diesel — media: rx6_fuel_meter.mp4

Every slow-down costs diesel to speed back up.

Nairobi → Malaba: +114 litres of diesel per loaded truck, ~300 kg CO₂.
Pretoria → Beitbridge: +28 litres.

Same distance. The difference is stop-and-go through roadside towns.

## Post 5 · safety — media: rx7_safety_sweep.mp4

One truck trip, Nairobi → Malaba: ~160,000 people passed at over 50 km/h, and 31 school stretches with no marked crossing.

Pretoria → Beitbridge: ~39,000 people, 2 school stretches.

The danger isn't in the towns. It's in the villages between them.
