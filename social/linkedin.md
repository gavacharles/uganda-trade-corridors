Uganda's trade roads have become village high streets. I measured what that costs.

Using only open data, I mapped every 500 m of the five main roads out of Kampala, to Malaba, Elegu, Katuna, Hoima and Bwera, about 1,700 km in all.

What stands out:

→ 56–74% of each corridor now runs through roadside settlement. Open road is down to 18–36%.
→ A loaded truck loses 59–161 minutes per trip to roadside activity, junctions, humps, police posts, weighbridges and hills: 29–64% on top of open-road time.
→ The roadside keeps filling in: 22–48% more buildings within 300 m of the roads between 2016 and 2023.
→ But the road is not the biggest delay. Trucks actually take 17–93 hours from Kampala to a border (Northern Corridor Transport Observatory). Road friction is only 2–14% of that; the rest is time stopped at borders, rest places and checks.
→ Stop-and-go driving burns about US$70 M of extra diesel a year, and 65–79% of road-safety exposure sits in the settlements between towns, where police place their crash black spots.

The cheapest wins are at controls: weigh-in-motion screening saves 8–16 truck minutes per trip, and ending routine police-post stops 4–15. Service roads through the worst settlement belts cut both delay and risk. And building control on the stretches that are still open matters now, before they become the next trading centre.

The animation: a loaded truck leaving Kampala on every corridor at once, next to the same truck on open road. The roads colour in with the minutes lost.
